# Lesson 1 — Chapter 1  
## How to read Hungarian when you're English-speaking  
### The alphabet and pronunciation

Learning to read Hungarian is much simpler than it seems for an English speaker.  
The good news: **Hungarian is pronounced exactly as it's written**.  
Once you know the pronunciation rules, there are (almost) no exceptions.

---

## 1. The Hungarian alphabet

The Hungarian alphabet has **44 letters**.  
It uses the Latin alphabet, but includes **accented letters** and **digraphs** (groups of two letters considered as one).

### Complete alphabet (in order)

A, Á, B, C, **CS**, D, **DZ**, **DZS**, E, É, F, G, GY, H, I, Í, J, K, L, LY, M, N, NY, O, Ó, Ö, Ő, P, R, S, SZ, T, TY, U, Ú, Ü, Ű, V, Z, ZS

> ⚠️ Groups like **CS**, **SZ**, **GY**, **NY** are separate letters in Hungarian.

---

## 2. Vowels

Hungarian distinguishes:
- **short vowels**
- **long vowels** (marked with an accent)

Length is **important**: it can change the meaning of a word.

### Simple vowels

| Letter | Pronunciation (English) | Example |
|------|--------------------------|---------|
| A | like *o* in "hot" | *ház* (house) |
| Á | like *a* in "father" (long) | *város* (city) |
| E | like *e* in "bed" | *ember* (person) |
| É | like *ay* in "day" (long) | *kék* (blue) |
| I | like *ee* in "see" | *kis* (small) |
| Í | like *ee* in "see" (long) | *ír* (to write) |
| O | like *o* in "rope" | *bor* (wine) |
| Ó | like *o* in "rope" (long) | *jó* (good) |
| U | like *oo* in "food" | *út* (road) |
| Ú | like *oo* in "food" (long) | *túl* (beyond) |

---

### Rounded vowels (unfamiliar to English speakers)

| Letter | Approximate pronunciation |
|------|-----------------------------|
| Ö | like *u* in "fur" |
| Ő | like *u* in "fur" (long) |
| Ü | like German *ü* (say *ee* with rounded lips) |
| Ű | like German *ü* (long) |

💡 Tip: for **ü**, say *ee* while rounding your lips.

---

## 3. Consonants

### Simple consonants (easy for English speakers)

B, D, F, H, K, L, M, N, P, R (slightly rolled), T, V, Z  
→ Pronunciation similar to English.

---

### Special consonants (essential to know)

| Letter | Pronunciation | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| C | ts | *cica* (cat) |
| CS | ch (like *church*) | *család* (family) |
| S | sh (like *shop*) | *sok* (much) |
| SZ | s (like *sing*) | *szép* (beautiful) |
| ZS | zh (like *measure*) | *zseb* (pocket) |
| J | y (like *yes*) | *jó* (good) |
| LY | y (like *yes*) | *lyuk* (hole) |
| GY | dy (soft d+y) | *gyerek* (child) |
| NY | ny (like Spanish *ñ*) | *anya* (mother) |
| TY | ty (soft t+y) | *tyúk* (chicken) |

⚠️ Pay attention:
- **S = sh**
- **SZ = s**  
(This is the most common mistake for English speakers.)

---

## 4. Double letters

A doubled consonant is **pronounced longer**.

| Example | Pronunciation |
|-------|--------------|
| *kappa* | the *p* is prolonged |
| *ssz* | long s |

Same for digraphs:  
**ssz**, **ccs**, **ggy**, etc.

---

## 5. Stress accent

In Hungarian, the stress is **always on the first syllable**.

| Word | Pronunciation |
|----|---------------|
| *Budapest* | **BU**-da-pest |
| *magyar* (Hungarian) | **MA**-gyar |

🎯 No exceptions.

---

## 6. Key points

- Hungarian is read **as it's written**
- Accents indicate **length**, not stress
- Digraphs are **single letters**
- Stress is **always at the beginning**
